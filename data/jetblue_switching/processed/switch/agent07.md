# Agent 07: Passenger price-sensitivity, loyalty and switching surveys, plus JetBlue Mosaic share

All quotes come from pages as fetched on 2026-11-10. A WebFetch summarizer extracted them, so check the wording against the source before putting it on a slide.

## 1. OAG Traveler Survey 2023 ("Loyalty and Disruption"). Fieldwork April 2023, about 2,000 North American travelers (the methodology section says 1,718 respondents)
URL: https://www.oag.com/traveler-survey-2023 (also https://www.travelagentcentral.com/your-business/oag-survey-travel-habits-loyalty-shaped-age-and-disruption)
- Switched airline loyalty in the past year: 37% of all travelers. By generation: Millennials 47%, Gen Z 37%, Gen X 23%, Boomers 15%.
- Delays or cancellations as a reason for switching: 37% of all travelers. "Thirty-seven percent of all travelers changed their airline loyalty due to having experienced significant delays"
- Cost as the main driver of preference and loyalty changes: 60%. "Gen Z (77 percent) are the most likely to change airline preferences to find flights that suit their budget"
- On-time performance as the top reason for staying loyal: 36% ("36 percent of all travelers saying it is the number-one reason for their continued loyalty")
- Implication: ABOVE 10%. This is self-reported annual churn across all carriers, not churn on shared routes. Reliability is the main loyalty lever, which supports the on-time 77.6% vs 71.0% argument.

## 2. GfK for TheStreet (2014), via SmarterTravel, 2014-08-19
URL: https://www.smartertravel.com/airline-loyalty-not-much/
- "63 percent of Americans have no loyalty to any particular airline"
- "5 percent of respondents said miles were their primary focus when choosing an airline"
- Price ranked first among choice factors; nonstop service was next at 28%.
- Implication: the pool of passengers who could switch is far above 10%. The data is old.

## 3. McKinsey, "The eight myths of airline retailing," 2025-05-22 (global, not US only)
URL: https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/travel/our-insights/the-eight-myths-of-airline-retailing
- "33 percent of respondents cite price as their top booking criterion"
- Conjoint analysis: "price holds the highest relative weight (34 percent)". Baggage allowance 16%, seat selection 10%, flexibility 9%.
- Implication: neutral to supportive. Price is the largest single driver, but product attributes carry the remaining 66% of the weight, so a product that is better at the same price can win share.

## 4. J.D. Power 2025 North America Airline Satisfaction Study, 2025-05-07 (study period March 2024 to March 2025). COUNTER-EVIDENCE
URL: https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250507575925/en/ ; https://www.fox2detroit.com/news/best-worst-airlines-customer-satisfaction-2025
- Economy/basic economy: Southwest 694, "JetBlue Airways (663) ranks second", Delta 662. United is not in the top 3. I did not find United's score on a public page.
- First/business: "JetBlue Airways ranks highest ... with a score of 738". United is listed among the LOWEST in this segment.
- Premium economy: Delta 717, JetBlue 699 (2nd).
- Loyalty: among passengers whose experience was "perfect", 81% "definitely will" fly that airline again. For a "poor" experience the figure is 4%.
- Implication: BELOW 10%. Passengers rate JetBlue's product above United's in both economy and premium, so "a better product at the same price" is not what customers report. The 10% case has to rest on reliability, network and Wi-Fi, not on overall satisfaction.

## 5. OAG, 2024-06-04 (Gen Z loyalty release)
URL: https://www.oag.com/pressroom/gen-z-travel-habits-usher-in-new-future-for-airline-rewards-programs-oag-survey-reveals
- "Only 65% of Gen Z and 70% of Millennials report being enrolled in airline frequent flyer programs" (Boomers 89%, Gen X 80%)
- Younger travelers are "27% more likely to pay up to $100 more" for a legacy carrier ticket than for a low-cost carrier or ULCC.
- Implication: mildly supportive. Younger travelers are willing to pay a premium for a legacy carrier.

## 6. Wi-Fi as a reason to choose an airline: Viasat annual passenger survey, about 11,000 respondents, global (PaxEx.Aero, 2024-12-10). TIME SERIES
URL: https://paxex.aero/viasat-inflight-connectivity-survey-results-2024
- "75% of passengers claimed they'd be more likely to select or rebook with an airline if quality inflight Wi-Fi was provided"
- Series: 2018 67%, 2022 82%, 2023 83%, 2024 75%. This can be plotted as a line graph.
- Older data: Inmarsat (Feb 2017, about 9,000 passengers in 18 countries): "44 percent of passengers would stop using their preferred airline in the next year" over poor connectivity. https://apex.aero/articles/44-pax-stop-flying-preferred-airline-due-poor-connectivity
- Caveat: these are stated preferences, global rather than US, and the question wording was not disclosed. They overstate real switching.

## 7. JetBlue Mosaic and elite share
- No public figure for Mosaic elites as a share of all JetBlue passengers or revenue was found.
- Q3 2026 earnings call (2026-10-27), Marty St. George, https://www.webull.com/news/15821575681355776 and https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/jetblue-first-class-pent-up-demand-premium-experiences/872955/ (2026-10-28):
  - "Mosaic members account for nearly a quarter of the seats booked". This refers to BlueFirst (the new domestic first class) only, not to all seats.
  - "Approximately 35% of bookings so far are award redemptions" (also BlueFirst)
  - Blue Sky: "more than 1.1 billion TrueBlue points redeemed on United flights since the loyalty assets launched in February". This is direct evidence that TrueBlue value is being spent on United. It is a points figure, not passenger counts or revenue.
  - "record levels of active TrueBlue members and elite status qualifications this quarter"
- Loyalty revenue "up 8% year over year in fiscal 2025" (CX Dive, 2026-04-06).
- Inference (mine, not sourced): Mosaic is about 25% of seats even in first class, the most elite-heavy cabin, so the elite share of total passengers is presumably far lower. That leaves most of the passenger base non-elite and less locked in.

## Bottom line
- The pool of passengers open to switching is large: 37% switched loyalty in a year (OAG 2023), 63% have no loyalty (GfK 2014), and 60% say cost drives their changes. This supports 10% as a plausible or even conservative share for a same-price product.
- J.D. Power 2025 cuts against the premise: JetBlue is rated above United in economy and in first/business. This argues for a rate below 10% unless reliability and Wi-Fi are the deciding factors.
