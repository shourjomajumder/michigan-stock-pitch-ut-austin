# Agent 09: Product/quality gaps at equal price and market share

Angle: United Next / Delta premium / American losing share / on-time and satisfaction vs share.
Searches used: 5 of 5. Everything below is quoted or paraphrased from fetched pages; my own arithmetic is labelled.

## Summary verdict
The cleanest public share-shift series (Chicago O'Hare, United vs American) shows share moving about 1-1.5 points per carrier per year. But that shift came mostly from capacity and gates, not from product at equal price. The academic evidence on quality-driven switching is real, but the effects are small: about 1% of volume per minute of delay, and about $1.21 per round trip per on-time point. Together these support a switching rate below 10% per year from product alone. A 10% cumulative shift over 1-2 years sits at the high end of the plausible range.

## 1. Chicago O'Hare: United vs American local-passenger share lead (best time series)
- Boltflight, citing United's CCO Andrew Nocella on the Q1 2025 earnings call: United reported a "22-point lead in local origin passenger share" over American. That lead was up from 6 points in 2019 and a negative gap in 2015.
  URL: https://boltflight.com/how-united-airlines-and-american-airlines-chicago-ohare-dominance-is-changing/ (undated page; quotes the Q1 2025 call, April 2025)
- AviationPros, Feb 17, 2026: United's lead over No. 2 grew from "a four-point deficit" 10 years ago to a "20-point gap" now.
  URL: https://www.aviationpros.com/ground-support-worldwide/airlines/news/55358005/american-has-little-choice-but-to-fight-this-fight-the-battle-with-united-for-chicago
- Same article: the two carriers "will account for about 85% of the market share at O'Hare this month" (Diio by Cirium). [broker estimate removed] United "earned $500 million of profit from its Chicago operation in 2025."
- Line-graph points (United lead minus American, in share points): 2015-16 about -4; 2019 +6; Q1 2025 +22; Feb 2026 about +20.
- My arithmetic: the lead swung 16 points from 2019 to Q1 2025 (about 5.25 years), or about 3 points of lead per year. That works out to roughly 1.5 share points per year per carrier.
- Caveat: the cause was mainly capacity. Over 2019-2023 American cut O'Hare flights 33.9% vs United's 23.9% (Cirium via Boltflight). Cranky Flier (Apr 21, 2026) says "Post-pandemic, United really took advantage of American's market neglect." American's share partly recovered once it added capacity back. American's seats were up 21% summer 2025 YoY (TPG, May 1, 2025), and the lead narrowed from 22 to about 20 points.
  URLs: https://crankyflier.com/?p=45808 ; https://thepointsguy.com/news/american-airlines-chicago-ohare-rebuild/
- Product was a factor too. United reopened its "improved and expanded Polaris Lounge,". [broker estimate removed]

## 4. Academic: quality and switching
- Suzuki (2000), Transportation Research Part E 36(2): 139-154: "once experiencing flight delays, passengers are more likely to switch airlines"; on-time performance "affects a carrier's market share primarily through the passengers' experience." The abstract gives no numeric estimate.
  URL: https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/transe/v36y2000i2p139-154.html
- Britto, Dresner & Voltes, "The Impact of Flight Delays on Passenger Demand and Consumer Welfare" (WCTR Lisbon abstract): "We find that flight delays on a route reduce passenger demand and raise airfares." The lagged-delay coefficient in the log-passenger equation is -0.01 (significant at 1%). That implies about -1% passengers per extra minute of average delay the next quarter. This is a reading of their table, not a figure the authors state.
  The paper also cites Morrison & Winston (1989): consumers value a 1-point on-time gain at about $1.21 per round trip.
  URL: https://wctrs-society.com/wp-content/uploads/abstracts/lisbon/general/01483.pdf
- My arithmetic: JetBlue's 6.6-point on-time gap vs United is worth about $8 per round trip at the 1989 valuation (not inflation-adjusted), roughly $20 in today's dollars. That is small relative to fares, which points to modest switching.
