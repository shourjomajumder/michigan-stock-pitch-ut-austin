# Agent 02: Alaska–American (WCIA), Alaska–Virgin America, Alaska–Hawaiian

## Bottom line
After 5 searches and about 15 fetches, I found **no published, measured customer-attrition rate** for Virgin America loyalists after the merger, and no measured shift in market share points on overlapping routes after the Alaska–American reciprocity (WCIA, 2020) or the Alaska–Hawaiian integration (closed Sep 2024). Alaska's own releases give only qualitative statements. The hard numbers available are about traffic flow under a codeshare, share versus product quality, capacity retreat, and fare effects.

## Findings (verbatim where quoted)

1. **The DOJ codeshare remedy halves partner-flow traffic.** Source: DOJ Competitive Impact Statement, *US v. Alaska Air Group & Virgin America*, Dec 6, 2016, https://www.justice.gov/atr/case-document/file/915986/dl
   - "roughly two-thirds of Virgin's network overlaps with American's network"
   - Before the remedy, Alaska could market American flights on over 250 routes.
   - The proposed Final Judgment would reduce the volume of Alaska passengers on American flights by about 50% (this is a paraphrase of the CIS, as returned by the fetch tool).
   - What it implies: the traffic a partnership sends to the partner carrier is large, and it shrinks quickly when the partnership terms change. This is not a switching rate as such. It is indirectly consistent with partnerships moving material volume, and it neither supports nor refutes 10%.

2. **A better product did not buy share against the hub carrier.** Source: Aviation Strategy, Apr 2017, https://aviationstrategy.aero/newsletter/Apr-2017/4/Alaska-Air-absorbs-Virgin-America's'great-emotional-energy'
   - "United dominates San Francisco with a 47% domestic market share."
   - Virgin America had "only a 7% share of the premium seats on the New York to San Francisco/Los Angeles routes" despite its well-regarded product.
   - The merger raised Alaska's SFO "customer relevance" from "9% (Alaska only) to 70%". California relevance reached 38% (120 nonstop markets), with "2m loyalty programme members" in California.
   - What it implies: a superior product alone produced low single-digit to single-digit share gains against an entrenched hub carrier. This argues that product-only switching runs **below 10%**. Network and loyalty breadth (what United offers JetBlue flyers) mattered more.

3. **Post-merger capacity retreat (seats, not customers).** Sources: Houston Chronicle, Chris McGinnis, 2018, https://preview-prod.w.houstonchronicle.com/chris-mcginnis/article/Alaska-Airlines-already-cutting-back-in-California-12736805.php ; Cranky Flier, 2023, https://crankyflier.com/?p=33533
   - "Combined with Virgin America, Alaska Airlines flew 12,357 seats per day SFO in 2017". Of these, Virgin flew 9,041 and Alaska 3,316.
   - In 2018 Alaska cut 5 SFO destinations: Minneapolis, Fort Lauderdale, Mexico City and Denver, plus a fifth.
   - "Alaska is currently 25 percent smaller at LAX in May 2023 than it was in May 2019"
   - What it implies: the acquired low-cost brand's California franchise shrank after integration. However, no source separates customer defection from Alaska's own capacity choices.

4. **Fare effects on overlap routes (context for diversion).**
   - Diana Li, Oct 9, 2024, https://dianali495.github.io/Working___Price_Effect_of_Alaska_Virgin_Merger.pdf: fares rose about +6.3% on direct-overlap routes and about +7.5% on potential-competition routes (matched controls). Average Alaska share on direct-overlap routes was 0.19.
   - Magnoni, MSc thesis, Erasmus, Jul 11, 2023, https://thesis.eur.nl/pub/66814/Master-Thesis-ESE-Max-Magnoni-658836.pdf: "6.5% ... increase in airfares" on overlapping routes, falling to about 3.3% in later specifications.
   - Neither paper reports passenger or share shifts. Both papers have only fare series, shown as figures with no tables.

5. **Alaska–Hawaiian.** Alaska's Q3 2025 release (Oct 23, 2025, https://news.alaskaair.com/company/alaska-air-group-reports-third-quarter-2025-results/) gives no share or loyalty-migration figures. It says only that co-brand card sign-ups "exceeded our year-end goal within two weeks". Airways (Dec 31, 2025) reports "over 55 million passengers" in the first combined year. No Hawaii market-share series was found in public pages.

## Time-series data
I found no usable quarterly or monthly passenger or share series from public pages. The closest options:
- SFO seats per day: 2017 (12,357 combined) versus the 2018 cuts. These are point estimates only.
- Building a real series would require DOT DB1B / T-100 data (SFO and LAX share for VX plus AS, 2015–2019). That is not done here.
