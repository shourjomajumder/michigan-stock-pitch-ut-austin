# Agent 01: American-JetBlue Northeast Alliance (NEA, 2021-2023)

Angle: DOJ trial record, D. Mass. opinion (Sorokin, J., May 19, 2023), expert material. Question: how much traffic/share moved between AA and JetBlue once reciprocal loyalty/codeshare started.

## Headline finding
The public record has NO direct passenger-switching rate, diversion ratio, or codeshare/loyalty uptake percentage between AA and JetBlue. These were in sealed expert reports (Miller for DOJ, Town reply, Israel and Carlton for defendants) and did not make it into the opinion or public briefs. What is public bounds the effect from above and below.

## Numbers (verbatim, with sources)

1. Size of the whole partnership effect on JetBlue revenue (the unwind)
   - JetBlue Q3 2023 earnings call (Oct 31, 2023), Dave Clark: "the NEA for the first -- for the third quarter was about a one-point headwind" ... "It grows to about two points this quarter, which we expect to be the biggest impact".
   - Source: https://allmind.ai/earnings/event/bt_JBLU_2023_Q3
   - Read: losing a full alliance (codeshare + reciprocal loyalty + revenue sharing + coordinated schedules) cost JetBlue about 1-2% of revenue. A loyalty-only partnership with no revenue sharing (Blue Sky) should move less than a full joint venture. Implies BELOW 10% on a system basis.

2. Incremental passengers attracted by the NEA (defendants' expert, Dr. Mark Israel)
   - Defendants' pretrial brief (Sept 24, 2022), p. 22: the NEA is "already attracting more than 3.3 million incremental passengers annually," worth "$511 to $610 million in consumer benefits"; projected "about 5.7 million passengers annually" once fully implemented (~"$635 million").
   - Source: https://appliedantitrust.com/14_merger_litigation/cases_doj/american_jetblue2021/1_dmass/american_jetblue_dmass_pretrial_brief_def2022_09_24.pdf
   - Caveat: a party's expert estimate, contested by DOJ; "incremental" means new to the two carriers combined, not switched from JetBlue to AA. My own rough arithmetic (not from the source): against the two carriers' combined NEA-region traffic of tens of millions (DOJ slides: 8.9M passengers in the Boston overlap markets and 23.1M in the NYC overlap markets, 2019), 3.3M is on the order of 10%. That is a gain from rivals, not a within-alliance shift.

3. Value of reciprocal loyalty to customers (trial testimony)
   - DOJ proposed findings (Nov 17, 2022), para 75, citing Kirby (AA President) testimony, Tr. Vol. 4 at 61:24-62:4: reciprocal AAdvantage access for TrueBlue customers "is likely to cause a five percent fare increase" for JetBlue flights.
   - Same source, para 74: JetBlue internal doc projected TrueBlue would grow at "20 percent" "absent AA/FFP deal" (PX0819 at 11). Para 75: JetBlue Loyalty Team estimated losses "over $100 million" (PX0723 at 8) (2020, before implementation).
   - Source: https://appliedantitrust.com/14_merger_litigation/cases_doj/american_jetblue2021/1_dmass/american_jetblue_dmass_pff_plf2022_11_17corrected.pdf
   - Read: reciprocal loyalty is worth about 5% in fare, so it is a real but modest pull. Neutral to slightly below 10%.

## Supporting context
- Overlap exposure, 2019 (DOJ PFF para 83): in JFK/LGA markets "57 percent of American passengers and 70 percent of JetBlue passengers" were on nonstop overlap routes. At BOS, 76% of AA's and 28% of JetBlue's were. This is the share of the base that could plausibly switch.
- Seat share shifts (capacity, not passengers), Aviation Week, May 23, 2023 (https://aviationweek.com/air-transport/airports-networks/analysis-unpicking-northeast-alliance):
  - JFK departure-seat share: JetBlue 22.4% (May 2019) to 25.4% (May 2023); AA 9.9% to 13.4%.
  - LGA (May 2023): AA 23%, JetBlue 11.5%, Delta 40.3%. JetBlue LGA seats "more than tripled" through AA slot leases, and AA was down ~50,000 monthly seats. This is a capacity reallocation of roughly 3-4 points of airport seat share, not organic passenger switching.
- Opinion (May 19, 2023; https://www.mtc.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/United-States-v-American-Airlines-Group-Inc-D.-Mass.-2023.pdf): AA block hours "increased by eight percent within the NEA region" and fell 12% elsewhere. Logan-LaGuardia nonstop carriers went from 3 to 2 after AA exited, with JetBlue taking the route. Revenue sharing meant JetBlue owed a 2021 transfer "upwards of $200 million" (capped at $27M). No switching percentages appear.
- DOJ harm (closing slides Nov 18, 2022, https://www.justice.gov/atr/case-document/file/1552921/download, citing Miller PX0461): "$696 Million Of Harm To Consumers". Defendants' growth with vs. without the NEA was "At most about 1%" for 2021.
- JetBlue LGA departures after unwind (Q3'23 call): 52 a day at the NEA peak, then mid-40s, then about 30 from late March 2024.

## Time series usable for a line graph
- Public, plottable: JetBlue NEA revenue headwind of about 1 pt (Q3'23) and about 2 pts (Q4'23, peak). JetBlue LGA daily departures: 52 (peak), about 45 (winter 23/24), about 30 (from late Mar 2024).
- Exists but not extractable: Town Reply Report Exs. 24-25 (DOJ slides 37-38), monthly ASMs Sep-2017 to Sep-2022 for AA/JetBlue, Delta and United, plus NEA vs. non-NEA JetBlue capacity. Only the axis labels are in the public PDF text, not the values.

## Bottom line for the 10% case
- A full joint venture, with revenue sharing and slot transfers on top of loyalty, was worth about 1-2% of JetBlue revenue on unwind, and about 5% in fare from loyalty reciprocity.
- Most of the visible share moves came from capacity handoffs (slot leases), not from customers choosing the partner.
- So NEA evidence supports a system-wide switching rate in the low single digits. 10% looks like an upper-end case that holds only on directly overlapping routes where the partner has the clearly better product.
