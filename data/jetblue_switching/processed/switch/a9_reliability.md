# a9_reliability: do reliability shocks move route share, and how fast? (DOT Table 6, YoY same-quarter changes)
- Southwest Dec 2022 meltdown (q0=2022Q4; 7 competitive city pairs where WN is largest or lowest-fare carrier: CHI-WAS, CHI-DFW, CHI-MCO, DEN-LAX, MCO-WAS, AUS-LAX, CHI-PHX). Mean WN share YoY: k0 -2.7pp (n6), k1 -1.8 (n7), k2 -0.3, k3 0.0, k4 +2.3, k5 +2.6 (base effect off a depressed year), k7 -1.2, k8 -1.4. First effect k0, peak k0-k1 (about 5-7% of a ~38% share), 50% recovered by k2, 90% by k3, no lasting loss. Weak signal: route-level SD about 4pp, so k0/k1 are only about 1.5 SE from zero. 2 of 7 routes (AUS-LAX, CHI-PHX) gained share throughout because WN was growing there.
- Matches Southwest's own figures: Q1 2023 revenue hit about $325M, concentrated in Jan-Feb, with managed business back near 2019 by March 2023 (WN 8-K; Axios). Bookings came back in about one quarter.
- Delta CrowdStrike outage, July 2024 (q0=2024Q3; ATL-NYC/WAS/CHI/DFW/HOU). Mean DL share YoY: k0 -0.2pp, k1 +2.0, k2 +1.4, k3 0.0, k4 -0.5. No measurable share loss. Delta reported a $380M revenue hit (refunds and compensation, not lost demand) and said "our completion factor leads the network carriers even when including the impact of the outage".
- American 2016-2018 operational problems (CHI-DFW only, q0=2016Q1 vs a 2015 average): AA YoY swings -7 to +7pp with no lasting trend. Not attributable to reliability. CLT-NYC was dropped because the city-name match returned two ambiguous series.
- JetBlue vs Delta at Boston, 2022-2026 (BOS-RDU, BOS-ATL, BOS-NYC; q0=2022Q1, pre-period = 2019 average). Moves are dominated by capacity: the NEA plus JetBlue's growth (B6 +8-9pp YoY on BOS-ATL in 2023 while DL fell 10-15pp, despite JetBlue's worse on-time record), then the NEA unwind and JetBlue's BOS-NYC cuts (B6 -7 to -11pp in 2025). No reliability-driven drift is separable from capacity.
- Timing for the model: an acute reliability shock gives an immediate, small (2-3pp), fully reversible loss within 2-3 quarters. A chronic reliability gap shows no Table 6 share drift once capacity is set aside. A reliability edge alone does not support a gradual, lasting 6% switch. Persistent switching would have to come from loyalty/product (Blue Sky), not on-time gaps.
- Caveats: Table 6 shows only the largest and the lowest-fare carrier per city pair, so series have gaps. Markets are city-level (multi-airport). Capacity is not controlled except by route choice. 2021-2022 COVID recovery distorts the WN and B6 pre-periods. n is small (5-7 routes per event). Spirit/JetBlue 2022-23 ops problems were not separately tested (Spirit is rarely observable in Table 6).
