# Agent 08: partnership analogs (share changes on overlapping routes)

Note: I used 6 WebSearch calls (1 over the 5 cap). All numbers come from public pages fetched 2026-11-10. Where the fetch tool would only paraphrase, I say so. Treat those as close summaries, not verbatim quotes.

## 1. Northwest/KLM alliance (GAO, 1995). Best "share-point" analog
- Source: GAO/RCED-95-99, *International Aviation: Airline Alliances Produce Benefits, but Effect on Competition Is Uncertain*, Apr 6, 1995. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GAOREPORTS-RCED-95-99/html/GAOREPORTS-RCED-95-99.htm
- Verbatim: "Northwest and KLM data show that their annual ridership has increased by about 350,000 as a result of their alliance," "producing an increase in their combined transatlantic market share from 7 percent in 1991 to 11.5 percent in 1994."
- Interior-city markets (34 US x 30 Europe/Middle East): share "from 1.2 percent in 1991 to 3.3 percent in 1994."
- What it means: +4.5 points over 3 years (about 1.5 pts/yr) on a 7% base. That is a 64% relative gain for the partnership, with fully integrated code-share plus antitrust immunity. On a ~1–2 year window, that is about 1.5–3 share points taken from all rivals combined.
- Time points (line graph): 1991 = 7.0%, 1994 = 11.5%. Interior markets: 1991 = 1.2%, 1994 = 3.3%.

## 2. USAir/British Airways (GAO, 1995). Direct "rival loses passengers" numbers
- Same GAO source.
- Code-share passengers "grew from 6,589 (between April and December 1993) to 47,749 (between April and December 1994)". Interline passengers rose "by 60 percent, from 36,396 to 58,164."
- Rivals' feed to BA, Apr–Dec 1994 vs 1993 (GAO, paraphrased by the fetch tool; one fragment is verbatim: "declined by 15 percent; the number of Delta's passengers declined by 12 percent"): United -15%, Delta -12%, Northwest -9%, TWA -6%, Continental -5%, American about flat. Airlines said the lost traffic moved to the USAir/BA code-share.
- One unnamed US carrier: passengers between 8 interior US cities and the UK where it competed with the alliance fell "about 11 percent between 1992 and 1993," versus -3% on its overall US–UK traffic (paraphrased). The excess loss is about 8 points, and the alliance began mid-period in May 1993.
- Delta "lost about $25 million in 1994 to the alliance". Another carrier lost over $40M, which is "11 percent of that airline's transatlantic operating revenues."
- What it means: when a partnership offered a better connecting product, rival carriers lost 5–15% of exposed traffic within about 1 year. That brackets our 10% case. Caveat: this is connecting/interline feed, which switches more easily than nonstop local traffic, and it ran in the opposite direction to our thesis (the alliance gained, the outsiders lost).

## 3. Hawaiian vs Southwest interisland (product/entry shock, not partnership). Upper bound
- Source: Aviation Week (Chris Sloan), Aug 29, 2023, citing OAG ASM share via TD Cowen's Helane Becker. https://aviationweek.com/air-transport/airlines-lessors/approaching-its-95th-anniversary-hawaiian-buffeted-challenges
- Hawaiian interisland ASM share fell from 87% (Aug 2019) to 63% (Aug 2022). Southwest rose from 10% to 33.5%. (Fetch-tool extraction; figures as reported.)
- Civil Beat, Mar 9, 2023: "Hawaiian still dominates neighbor island business, with 67.6% of neighbor island seats." In Q3 2022, "Hawaiian's load factor ... was 22 percentage points higher than Southwest's," so Southwest's capacity share overstates its passenger share. https://civilbeat.org/2023/03/hawaiian-vs-southwest-good-news-for-travelers-bad-news-for-the-bottom-line/
- What it means: an aggressive capacity entry moved about 24 capacity points in 3 years (about 8/yr). That is a ceiling for a pure capacity war. Passenger share moved less. Blue Sky adds no capacity, so this bounds the switching rate from above (roughly 20%+ is implausible in 1–2 yrs).
- Time points: Aug 2019 = 87/10, Aug 2022 = 63/33.5 (Hawaiian/Southwest ASM share).

## 4. Delta–WestJet (DOT show-cause order, Oct 13, 2020). Context only
- https://www.transportation.gov/sites/dot.gov/files/2020-10/Delta%20WestJet%20Show%20Cause%20Order%202020-10-13.pdf
- Pre-JV transborder capacity shares: Air Canada 45% (57% with United), WestJet 15%, United 12%, Delta 11%, "Delta/WestJet would have a combined 27 percent share." The only nonstop overlap was NYC–Toronto (WestJet 15%, Delta 6%).
- 2014–19 seat growth: Air Canada +52%, Delta +42%, WestJet +14%, United -24%. WestJet was the weaker carrier that lost relative share while its partner grew (fetch-tool table).
- The JV with ATI was abandoned in Nov 2020 after DOT conditions, so there is no post-JV share data. Not a usable switching measure.

## 5. Delta–LATAM. Pre-deal baseline only
- Travel Weekly (Oct 2019, article undated; uses Sept 2019 OAG): US–South America shares were American 26.3%, LATAM 20.6%, United 11.8%, Delta 8%, and Delta+LATAM would have "28.6%." https://www.travelweekly.com/Aviation/Delta-Latam-partnership-shakes-up-South-America-market/324942
- Delta press release, Oct 20, 2025: JV combined capacity up 88% (ASKs) in 3 years, with 14.5M passengers. There is no share figure. https://news.delta.com/delta-e-latam-comemoram-tres-anos-de-parceria-com-crescimento-de-88-em-capacidade-e-conectividade
- I found no public post-JV share series.

## 6. American–JetBlue NEA (D. Mass. opinion, May 19, 2023). Little share data
- https://uschamber.com/assets/documents/District-Court-Decision.pdf
- American's block hours "had increased by eight percent within the NEA region" but "had decreased by twelve percent across the rest of American's system." The first ~100k characters had no before/after share or loyalty-switching figures.

## 7. GAO-19-237 (Mar 2019)
- Immunized carriers "provided approximately 75 percent of the available seats on trans-Atlantic flights" (2017). It reports no share-change numbers. GAO says DOT does not systematically measure post-ATI effects.

## Not found (within budget)
Delta–Virgin Atlantic post-JV share, United–Air Canada share changes, Southwest–AirTran overlap share, Spirit/Frontier reactions, and Alaska–Hawaiian post-merger share. None had public quantitative share-shift data that I could reach.

## Bottom line for the 10% case
- Partnership analogs show partnerships gaining about 1.5 share points per year (NW/KLM) and rivals losing 5–15% of exposed traffic within about 1 year (USAir/BA). Both are consistent with 10% over 1–2 years. But they ran with full code-share/ATI and moved connecting traffic, which is stronger than Blue Sky's loyalty-only link.
- Pure capacity entry (Southwest in Hawaii) moved about 8 capacity points per year. That is a ceiling, and it suggests 20% is the outer edge.
- Caveat: in all these analogs the partnership gained against outsiders. None measures one partner losing passengers to the other partner, which is our mechanism.
