Found: Bamberger-Carlton-Neumann (2004; public NBER conference draft) and Gayle (2007 draft, DL/CO/NW) report only AGGREGATE city-pair-panel effects, not named-route shares or dollar fares; GAO/RCED-99-37 gives one static route share (Detroit-Newark 97% NW+CO, 1997).
Key numbers: CO/HP (1994Q3 to 1995Q3) partner traffic about +35% vs rivals +3.5%, partner fares -8.5% vs rival -6.5%, partner share ~17% post; NW/AS (1994Q3 to 1996Q3) rival traffic -7.7% to -8.2% (significant), partner traffic +1.8-2.9% (insignificant); DL/CO/NW (2002Q4 to 2003Q4) partner traffic +17.2% vs -2.6% on non-alliance pairs with fares roughly flat.
Parity routes: 0 confirmed. No source gives route-level partner-vs-rival fare levels; NW/AS is explicitly non-parity (partners ~30% cheaper than rivals); DL/CO/NW is the closest analog (fares ~flat, volume shifts to partners) but has no rival fare levels.
Caveats: all effects are difference-in-differences vs non-alliance city pairs, mostly connecting/interline-to-online markets, so gains are largely new online service rather than share-shift on a shared nonstop route; rivals lost share but not volume in CO/HP.
Caveats 2: share_before never reported (BCN fn 18 says combined partner share rose significantly, no magnitude); depth=2 is assumed since fetched texts confirm codeshare but not loyalty reciprocity; Armantier-Richard (CO/NW) full text not reachable (paywalled), only qualitative summary seen; numbers read via page-summarising fetch of the PDFs, direct download was blocked.
