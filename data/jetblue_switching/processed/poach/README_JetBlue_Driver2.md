# JetBlue Driver 2: United Poaches JetBlue Flyers Through Blue Sky
Hook Em – Michigan Ross pitch (short JetBlue). Final data package, Nov 8, 2026.

## 1. The thesis in one line
United and JetBlue's Blue Sky partnership lets JetBlue flyers try United with no cost to their miles or status. United is the better product, so on the routes both airlines fly, some JetBlue flyers will switch to United.

## 2. Bottom line (use on the slide)
- History says the effect is real but small: about **+0.5 to +1.2 points** of extra passenger share to the bigger partner, appearing **1–4 quarters** after the partnership starts and fading within **1–3 years**.
- **No switching shows up yet** in DOT data through July 2026. United's gains so far come from JetBlue cutting routes, not from flyers choosing United.
- Sizing through Q4 2027: **Bear ~$3M, Base ~$14M, Bull ~$39M** of lost JetBlue revenue.
- First proof point: **DOT T-100 data for Q4 2026 (out ~March 2027) and Q1 2027 (out ~June 2027).**

## 3. Key terms (for whoever presents)
- **Load factor** = share of seats filled with paying passengers.
- **"Points of extra passenger share"** = on routes both airlines fly, the partner's share of passengers minus its share of seats. If United flies 50% of seats but carries 51% of passengers, its planes are running fuller than JetBlue's, so flyers are choosing United beyond the seats it added: +1 point.
- **Control** = a non-partner airline on the same routes over the same quarters, subtracted out to remove general trends (difference-in-differences).

## 4. Historical cases (Switching_history_lefthalf.png)

### Case 1: Delta & Alaska (Seattle, 2004–2017) – strongest evidence
- **Alliance:** shared codes, miles and elite perks; Delta then built its own Seattle hub (2013–2015).
- **Effect:** on routes both flew, Delta's planes ran fuller (higher load factor) than Alaska's, so Delta carried a bigger share of passengers than its share of seats.
- **Why:** Alaska flyers could try Delta at no cost to miles or status.
- **Size:** +0.6 pts after ~4 quarters; peak +1.1 to +1.2 pts after ~6–7 quarters; gone after ~3 years (Alaska fought back with bonus miles and cut perks; partnership ended May 2017).

### Case 2: American & JetBlue (Northeast Alliance, 2021–2023) – weaker evidence
- **Alliance:** shared codes, miles (May 2021) and elite perks (Oct 2021); court ended it July 2023.
- **Effect:** American's planes ran fuller than JetBlue's on shared routes right after elite perks started.
- **Why:** JetBlue flyers kept miles and perks on American's much bigger network.
- **Size:** about +1.2 pts within 1–2 quarters, faded within ~1 year. Weaker because the gap did not reverse when the alliance ended.

### Other precedents (for Q&A or a footnote)
- **Southwest & ATA (Chicago Midway, 2005–2008):** Southwest's first codeshare. It gave bankrupt ATA $117M for Midway gates, then took most of its Chicago business; ATA quit Midway domestic flying in 2008. Extreme case, driven by capacity more than flyer choice; use as a downside scenario, not the base case.
- **Academic research:** Lederman (MIT / RAND Journal of Economics) finds frequent-flyer partnerships raise an airline's market share, more so at airports where it is dominant (United dominates Newark, where most shared routes start). Gayle finds partner traffic rose 19.8–24.4% on alliance city pairs, but only where the partners already held >49% of the market.

## 5. Today's shared routes
13–14 nonstop routes a quarter that both United and JetBlue fly:
BOS–ORD, EWR–LAX, EWR–PBI, EWR–FLL, BOS–IAH, EWR–RSW, BOS–DEN, EWR–TPA, EWR–SJU, EWR–MCO, FLL–SFO, BOS–LAX, BOS–SFO, EWR–LAS.
- ~2.73M passengers a quarter across both airlines (2025 average: United ~2.0M, JetBlue ~0.74M).
- Average one-way fare ~$284 (DOT Consumer Airfare Report, Q1 2026, passenger-weighted).
- **1 point of share ≈ 27,300 passengers a quarter ≈ $7.8M of revenue a quarter.**

## 7. Why switching could still pick up
- **Product gap:** Jan–Jun 2026 on-time arrivals: United 77.6% (3rd of 9) vs JetBlue 71.0% (8th of 9). July 2026 cancellations: JetBlue 9.8% vs United 2.7% (DOT data via AeroCorner).
- **Status credit flows one way:** JetBlue TrueBlue members earn Mosaic credit on United flights, but United MileagePlus members earn no status credit on JetBlue flights. Switching is free toward United, not the other way.
- **Counterpoint to be ready for:** J.D. Power ranked JetBlue #1 for premium cabins and #3 in economy in 2026.

## 8. Timeline and catalysts
- Oct 23, 2025: reciprocal mile earning starts.
- Week of Feb 10, 2026: cross-booking starts.
- Week of May 14, 2026: reciprocal elite benefits start (the trigger).
- Expected effect window (Delta–Alaska timing, 1–4 quarters after): **Q4 2026 – Q2 2027.**
- **Catalyst 1:** DOT T-100 data for Q4 2026, out ~March 2027.
- **Catalyst 2:** DOT T-100 data for Q1 2027, out ~June 2027.
- **Catalyst 3:** United's own JFK launch in 2027.

## 9. Suggested slide line
"When Delta and Alaska were loyalty partners, Delta took about 1 point of Alaska's passenger share beyond the seats it added. Blue Sky has the same setup, plus a status rule that favors United. Our base case is ~$14M of lost JetBlue revenue through 2027, visible in the Q4'26–Q1'27 DOT data."

## 10. Caveats (be upfront in Q&A)
- Small: roughly 0.2% of JetBlue's revenue. A supporting point, not a main driver.
- Only one of the precedents (Delta–Alaska) shows it clearly.
- Delta–Alaska includes heavy Delta capacity growth; the control only partly removes it.
- T-100 counts the operating airline only, so regional affiliates are excluded.
