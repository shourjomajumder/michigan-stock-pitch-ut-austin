# 7. Cross-airport alliance / loyalty precedents (multi-airport metros)

Question: is there precedent for a partnership or loyalty tie-up pulling passengers ACROSS airports in the same metro (JFK flyer -> EWR)? Short answer: I found **no public number that directly measures alliance-driven cross-airport switching**. The best precedents point the other way: in New York, loyal customers did not follow their own airline from JFK to EWR, and the econometric work treats EWR as a weaker substitute for JFK/LGA than an airline at the same airport. The evidence supports counting the EWR overlap only with a large haircut.

Sources were accessed 2026-11-11. Quotes are under 15 words.

---

## 1. United's own JFK exit (2015): loyal premium flyers did NOT follow to Newark  (cuts AGAINST)
- United moved its JFK transcons (LAX/SFO) to EWR in Oct 2015. Its own MileagePlus customers had the same airline, the same program and better schedule depth at EWR.
- In 2017 United President Scott Kirby said at a Newark town hall: "I wish I could roll back the clock and change the decision." He said many of the most lucrative West Coast customers want New York City, not New Jersey, and United lost some of them.
  - Skift, Apr 21, 2017: https://skiftstaging.dev/?p=228532 (mirror of the Skift article "United Airlines President: Leaving New York's JFK 'Was the Wrong Decision'")
- CNBC (Mar 28, 2021) on United's return to JFK: Kirby called the exit a mistake because it let American win lucrative corporate clients.
  - https://www.cnbc.com/2021/03/28/united-airlines-to-jfk-airport-as-covid-19-lull-ends-its-5-year-absence.html
- No public figure exists for how many customers were lost.
- **Relevance:** this is the cleanest natural experiment for "will a loyal flyer go to EWR instead of JFK?" Even with full loyalty (same airline, same status), a meaningful part of the high-value JFK base went to another JFK carrier rather than switch airports. Blue Sky gives a weaker loyalty pull than this, so the JFK-to-EWR transfer should be weaker still.

## 2. NEA trial (US v. American/JetBlue, D. Mass., decided May 2023): the court left the question open  (mixed)
- DOJ's expert (Miller) defined the New York markets as JFK/LGA only. The defendants argued New York must include Newark.
- Judge Sorokin called the dispute "much ado about nothing" and wrote that "both sides might be right." He did not decide it (opinion p. 76).
- 2019 seat shares: American plus JetBlue held over 30% at JFK+LGA but 25% with Newark included (p. 77). So adding EWR dilutes the JFK/LGA picture by about a fifth.
- The opinion has **no figures on passengers shifting between New York airports** under the NEA.
  - Opinion PDF: https://uschamber.com/assets/documents/District-Court-Decision.pdf (pp. 76-77)
- Trial testimony (Courthouse News, date shown on the page is Dec 6, 2023, but the testimony is from the fall 2022 trial):
  - Defendants' economist Darin Lee said "almost 18% of taxi and rideshare trips" between Manhattan and an airport go to Newark. DOJ's reply: "Over 80%" go to JFK/LGA.
  - Jan Brueckner, the defendants' expert, testified that fares at all three NYC airports move when a low-cost carrier adds capacity at one of them. That supports the airports being one market for fares.
  - DOJ confronted Brueckner with his own 2014 analysis, made to defend the United-Continental merger, which found Newark was **not** part of the New York market.
  - https://www.courthousenews.com/does-newark-airport-serve-new-york-city-billions-of-dollars-may-ride-on-the-answer/
- **Relevance:** regulators and the court treated JFK/LGA-only as a defensible market. Even the airlines' own expert had earlier excluded EWR. About 18% (Manhattan only) is a rough upper-end proxy for EWR's natural pull among city travelers. It would be lower from Queens, Brooklyn or Long Island, which is JetBlue's JFK catchment.

## 3. Academic market definition: adjacent airports are substitutes only case by case
- Brueckner, Lee & Singer (2014), "City-pairs versus airport-pairs," Review of Industrial Organization 44(1):1-25 (2003-09 panel). They found city-pairs fit better "for many, but not all" metros, and grouping must be decided case by case.
  - https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/revind/v44y2014i1p1-25.html
- Analysis Group summary of that method: DCA and BWI come out as substitutes, but SJC and SFO do not, despite being 35 miles apart.
  - https://analysisgroup.com/link/3ce4be30684f4ed8a5b75489e5b6f6da.aspx
- Per DOJ at the NEA trial, the 2014 work's NYC result excluded Newark (see section 2).

## 4. Choice-model magnitudes: loyalty is worth less than the extra trip to the other airport
- Hess, Adler & Polak (2007), Transportation Research Part E 43(3), US stated-preference survey (589 respondents, 5,890 choices):
  - Value of frequent-flyer membership versus none: business elite **$125**, business standard **$49**, leisure standard **$11** (not significant).
  - Value of airport access time: business **$75/hr**, leisure **$36/hr**.
  - Implied: standard membership is worth about 39 min of extra access time for business and about 19 min for leisure. Elite status is worth about 100 min for business. (My arithmetic.)
  - The authors note stated-preference surveys can overstate responsiveness.
  - https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/5435/1/Hesss6pdf.pdf
- Hess & Polak (2006), Papers in Regional Science 85(4), San Francisco Bay Area, 1995 MTC survey (5,091 observations):
  - Strong airport "inertia": past use of an airport is a large positive driver of choosing it again (business coefficients 1.18 for SFO to 2.00 for OAK).
  - Access time costs about $150/hr for resident business travelers (my calculation from the paper's coefficients).
  - https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/43626/5/Hess14.pdf
- Pels, Nijkamp & Rietveld (SF Bay Area): choices are best modeled as airport first, then airline, with access time significant. That means airlines at the same airport substitute for each other more closely than airlines at different airports.
  - https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/airport-and-airline-choice-in-a-multiple-airport-system-an-empiri/
- **Relevance:** the Blue Sky switcher is mostly a base-fare leisure flyer. For them, loyalty is worth about $11, or roughly 20 minutes of access time. Getting to EWR from JetBlue's Queens, Brooklyn or Long Island catchment typically adds well over 20 minutes. Under Blue Sky they also keep earning TrueBlue credit on United, so the switch is about product quality (Wi-Fi, on-time), not loyalty.

## 5. Other candidates checked: no usable cross-airport switching numbers found
- **Alaska-Virgin America (SFO vs OAK/SJC):** the court used city-pair framing, per Analysis Group. I found no public data on passengers moving between Bay Area airports because of the merger. The Brueckner method found SFO and SJC not substitutes.
- **London (LHR/LGW):** the CAA Gatwick market power appendix says Open Skies-driven switching from LGW to LHR "was unique," a legislative one-off, and gives no diversion figures.
  - https://www.caa.co.uk/media/2nwbusc2/gal-cap-1134-appendix-e-evidence-and-analysis-on-competitive-constraints-airlines-non-con-1.pdf
- **DC (DCA/IAD/BWI):** under the Brueckner method DCA and BWI come out as substitutes. At the NEA trial Brueckner excluded Dulles because "getting there is very inconvenient."
- **United-Continental (EWR):** DOJ required EWR slot transfers to Southwest (2010) and analyzed Newark on its own. No public cross-airport share-shift data found.
- **Southwest MDW/ORD, Delta-Alaska Seattle:** no public evidence found of loyalty-driven cross-airport movement.

## Best evidence for the pitch (1-3 bullets)
- The NEA court held that New York "might" properly include Newark ("both sides might be right," p. 76). Brueckner testified that fares at all three NYC airports respond to low-cost capacity at any one, so EWR does discipline JFK on price.
- About 18% of Manhattan taxi and rideshare airport trips already go to EWR (Lee testimony). A real slice of New York demand is airport-flexible.
- On the 19 overlap routes, United at EWR is a real alternative for flexible, price-indifferent travelers. Blue Sky removes the loyalty cost of switching, because TrueBlue credit is still earned on United.

## Evidence against
- **United's own 2015 JFK exit:** even fully loyal MileagePlus premium flyers did not follow to EWR. Many went to American at JFK, and Kirby publicly regretted the move. This is the strongest precedent, and it cuts against the pitch.
- DOJ, its expert, and Brueckner's own 2014 analysis treated JFK/LGA as a market that excludes EWR. Over 80% of Manhattan airport trips go to JFK/LGA.
- Choice models put airport choice ahead of airline choice. Loyalty for leisure flyers (about $11) is worth far less than the extra access time to EWR.
- No precedent found anywhere with a number showing an alliance or loyalty tie-up moving passengers across airports in the same metro.

## Suggested haircut and why
- Count the EWR-overlap passengers at about **15-25%** of the Boston same-airport rate. Central case: **20%**, i.e. 6.48M x 0.20 = about 1.3M effective passengers. This anchors on:
  - EWR's roughly 18% share of Manhattan airport trips, likely lower from JetBlue's eastern catchment;
  - the drop from over 30% to 25% share when EWR is included;
  - the United JFK-exit evidence that even loyal flyers do not cross.
- Optional refinement: give premium and business-heavy routes (LAX, SFO) a somewhat higher factor (around 30%), since United's product edge matters more there. Give leisure routes (MCO, FLL, SJU, PBI and similar) around 10-15%.
- Present the 100% (full EWR) figure only as an upside sensitivity, not the base case.
