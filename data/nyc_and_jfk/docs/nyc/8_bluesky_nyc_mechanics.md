Researched 2026-11-11. Public sources only. No browser test was run (Chrome tools reserved for agent 4; direct requests to jetblue.com were blocked by the proxy, HTTP 403). Every "live search behavior" point below is therefore UNVERIFIED and should be checked by agent 4 or by hand.

Bottom line: a JFK-based customer sees United's Newark nonstops only by typing EWR into the United booking flow (on jetblue.com or united.com), or by using a metasearch (Google Flights/Kayak "NYC"). Blue Sky does NOT automatically put EWR in front of a JFK searcher. That is an important caveat for the "NYC = one market" argument.

## 3. Reciprocal perks for Mosaic members on United (official table, jetblue.com/jetblue-united)

- Priority check-in, security, bag handling: all Mosaic tiers
- Priority boarding: Group 2 (Mosaic 1), Group 1 (Mosaic 2-4)
- 1 free checked bag: all tiers
- Preferred seats after booking; Economy Plus at check-in: all tiers
- Same-day standby: all tiers
- NOT included: lounge access, first-class upgrades (TPG 2026-02-10, 2026-05-15)
- Companions on the same reservation get priority check-in/security/boarding and standby. The Mosaic member plus 1 companion get a preferred seat. Bag and Economy Plus are member-only (YMMV 2026-08-07)
- Basic economy treatment: not addressed in any source found. Gap.
- Applies only to elites (Mosaic). A plain TrueBlue base member gets points only, with no seat, bag or boarding perks on United.

## 4. Interline, connections, airport transfer

- Press release: Blue Sky is an "interline agreement, not a codeshare". Each airline keeps its own flight numbers (JetBlue/United release, 2025-05-29, via finviz).
- Mixed-carrier single-ticket connections: still not live as of the latest sources (TPG 2026-05-15). Through-checked bags, misconnect protection and cross-airport (JFK<->EWR) transfers: nothing offered or announced. No source mentions any ground transfer.

## 5. New York statements and facts

- **JFK:** JetBlue gives United slots "for up to seven daily round-trip flights out of JFK Terminal 6 to begin as early as 2027" (press release 2025-05-29; repeated in DOT-review release, BusinessWire 2025-07-29). Kirby: "our employees are really excited about United's return to JFK" (OMAAT 2025-05-29).
- **Newark:** "JetBlue and United will exchange eight flight timings at Newark", described as net-neutral (same releases). View from the Wing (2025-05-29) disputes that the swap is "even".
- **DOT:** cleared review "without objection". The airlines say there is no fare or schedule coordination beyond the limited NY slots (OMAAT 2025-07-29).
- **JetBlue pulling back from Newark:**
  - OMAAT (2026-05-19): JetBlue cut 5 of 12 year-round EWR routes (AUA, CUN, PUJ, SDQ, TPA), effective 2026-07-08. Remaining: FLL, RSW, LAS, MCO, SJU, STI, PBI. Headline: "JetBlue Slashes Newark Flights, A Gift To Partner United".
  - News12 (2026-06-18): JetBlue closes its EWR inflight crew base and its EWR + LGA maintenance bases in fall 2026. It ends seasonal EWR-LAX and EWR-LAS, with aircraft moved to FLL.
  - Note: the MileagePlus no-earn exclusions (EWR to CUN, AUA, LAX, LAS, PUJ) were exactly the head-to-head EWR routes. JetBlue has since exited most of them.

## Best evidence for the pitch (1-3 bullets)
- JetBlue is visibly ceding Newark to United (5 of 12 EWR routes cut, crew base closed, OMAAT calls it a "gift to partner United"). The airlines are carving up NYC by airport, which supports treating NYC as one competitive market. Within it, United owns EWR, and JetBlue's JFK customers who would rather use EWR now have only United to pick.
- United arrives at JFK T6 (up to 7 daily round trips, as early as 2027). The overlap then becomes same-airport for those routes, so no airport-substitution haircut is needed there (likely transcon/long-haul; route list not announced).
- Mosaic elites get real United perks (free bag, Economy Plus, priority boarding) plus 8-10x points and status credit on United. These are the high-value customers most likely to switch.

## Evidence against
- jetblue.com does not appear to cross-sell EWR to a JFK searcher. There is no documented NYC multi-airport code; United's flights sit in a separate booking flow; united.com's own "NYC" reportedly defaults to EWR only. The switch needs the customer to choose a different airport on purpose. Blue Sky lowers the loyalty cost of switching but not the airport cost.
- Base (non-elite) TrueBlue members earn less on United (5x vs 6x) and get no perks, so for most passengers the loyalty pull toward United is weak.
- No through-ticketing or connections yet, and no airport transfer product.
- The airlines say there is no fare/schedule coordination, and JetBlue's EWR retreat also removes direct EWR overlap the model might otherwise count.

## Suggested haircut and why
Do not count JFK pax at 100%. Blue Sky gives JFK flyers no mechanism to see EWR. Substitution depends on (a) the customer's ground access to EWR vs JFK and (b) elite status, since perks apply only to Mosaic members. Suggest: apply the airport-substitution haircut from the other agents' cross-airport data (e.g., diversion/catchment-overlap share). Then, if desired, weight the loyalty effect more heavily for the Mosaic share of pax. Count JFK routes United will actually fly from T6 in 2027 at full weight once announced.
