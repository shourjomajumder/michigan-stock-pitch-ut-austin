# Agent 9: Devil's advocate. Why JFK passengers should NOT be counted 1:1 as switchable to United at Newark

All sources accessed 2026-11-11. Quotes kept under 15 words.

## 1. Flyers rank Newark last among the NYC airports, and access time drives airport choice

- **Kayak booking data, Reuters via AJOT (May 23, 2025):** https://www.ajot.com/news/demand-traffic-falls-at-newark-airport-following-outages
  - The Kayak CEO described the preference order as LaGuardia, then JFK, "and then Newark."
  - After the April 28, 2025 outage, travelers choosing Newark for arrival fell 19% and for departure about 15%. Newark arrivals fell about 13% year over year from Apr 28 to May 20.
  - Cuts against us: this shows flyers do move between NYC airports when one gets worse (see "Evidence against").
- **Ishii, Jun & Lee, airport choice in the SF Bay Area, a three-airport region (UC Irvine working paper):** https://www.economics.uci.edu/files/docs/workingpapers/2005-06/Ishii-22.pdf
  - Access time is the strongest driver. Business and leisure coefficients are almost identical: -0.0815 and -0.0774.
  - Cutting SFO access time by only 5 minutes raises SFO's share from 29.3% to 35.7%, which is +20% for business and +24% for leisure.
  - Leisure flyers showed a negative United preference (marginal effect -2.32, vs -0.65 for business).
  - Fares matter much more to leisure flyers (-0.091) than business flyers (-0.024, not significant).
  - Takeaway: "same product, better Wi-Fi" does not beat an extra 45+ minutes of ground travel.
- **Hess & Polak, London airports (White Rose eprint):** https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/5432/1/hesss5.pdf
  - Business travelers accepted only about 6 extra minutes of access time per additional flight frequency.
  - The paper cites the SF Bay studies for high access-time sensitivity, "especially for business travellers."
  - It adds that "airport captivity" plays a bigger role in SF Bay than in London.

## 2. The ground-access penalty from JetBlue's JFK catchment to Newark is large

- **MTA guide:** https://www.mta.info/guides/airports/newark-ewr
  - Midtown to EWR by NJ Transit plus AirTrain costs $16.80 and takes about 1 hour, plus 15-20 minutes to the terminal.
  - From Brooklyn or Lower Manhattan, PATH plus NJ Transit costs $14.95.
  - For comparison, JFK's AirTrain is $8.50 plus a subway fare.
- **Blade:** https://www.blade.com/ewr-to-jfk
  - EWR and JFK are about 35 road miles apart.
  - By car it takes 45-90 minutes; by transit, 75-120 minutes. An Uber costs $60-100.
- For Queens, Brooklyn and Long Island residents (JFK's natural catchment), the extra access time to EWR is roughly 45-90 minutes.
- Using the Ishii coefficients, that is roughly 9-18 times the 5-minute shock that moved share by about 20%.
- Assumption flag: I could not retrieve current Port Authority origin-zone survey data, because the FAA/PANYNJ 2007 study PDFs are blocked to automated fetch. The NJ vs NY origin split is therefore not quantified here.

## 3. Newark is less reliable than JFK, and capped until Oct 2027

- **AirHelp 2025 US disruption report:** https://img.airhelp.com/Documents/Reports/disruption-2025/usa

| Airport | On-time | Delayed | Cancelled |
|---|---|---|---|
| EWR | 71% | 26% | 2.7% |
| JFK | 73% | 25% | 1.6% |

  - EWR's cancellation rate is about 1.7 times JFK's.
- **USAFacts, BTS data for H1 2025:** https://usafacts.org/reports/facts-in-focus/best-worst-airports-air-travel-delays-ewr-ord-lga-bts
  - EWR had 70.8% on-time arrivals, the 2nd-worst of the 30 busiest US airports.
- **FAA order, Federal Register (June 23, 2026):** https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/23/2026-12589/operating-limitations-at-newark-liberty-international-airport
  - EWR is capped at 36 arrivals plus 36 departures per hour (72 operations), against the prior 77, through **Oct 30, 2027**.
  - The FAA cites "continued staffing deficiencies at EWR." Area C has 28 certified controllers against a target of 46 (60.8%).
  - EWR arrival on-time performance was 75% (Jun 2025 to Mar 2026).
  - Implication: United cannot add EWR frequency to absorb defecting JetBlue flyers before late 2027.
- **2025 outages:** The April 28, 2025 radar and telecom blackout lasted 30-90 seconds (AJOT link above). A further outage hit on May 9, 2025 (AirlineGeeks: https://airlinegeeks.com/2025/05/13/faa-plans-temporary-flight-reductions-at-newark/). Both made national news, which reinforces Newark's reputation.

## 4. DOT data shows JFK and EWR are different fare markets (Table 1a / Table 7 vs Table 1)

- **DOT Domestic Airline Fares Consumer Report, Q2 2025:** https://www.transportation.gov/sites/dot.gov/files/2025-09/Domestic%20Airline%20Fares%20Consumer%20Report%202025_Q2.pdf
  - Table 1 pools all three airports as "New York City, NY (Metropolitan Area)." Table 1A reports airport pairs separately.
  - Airport-level figures from Table 7:

| Airport | Avg fare | Fare premium | Passengers in low-fare markets |
|---|---|---|---|
| JFK | $294 | 13% | 88% |
| EWR | $269 | 11% | 66% |

- The JetBlue-United fare-parity premise was checked on Boston airport pairs. A NYC city-pair average blends JFK, LGA and EWR, so it cannot show parity between a JFK fare and an EWR fare.
- Any parity claim for these 19 routes must be verified on JFK-X vs EWR-X rows in Table 1a. I could not pull that dataset because catalog.data.gov is blocked from this sandbox.

## 5. JetBlue's loyalty is growing, not leaking (Q3 2026)

- **JetBlue Q3 2026 release, Oct 26, 2026:** https://www.financialcontent.com/article/bizwire-2026-10-26-jetblue-announces-third-quarter-2026-results
  - "Realized loyalty revenue growth of 20% year-over-year."
  - Co-brand spending had double-digit growth.
  - Premier Card acquisitions grew more than eightfold.
  - Members have redeemed more than 1.1B TrueBlue points on United flights.
- Judge's reading: Blue Sky is deepening TrueBlue engagement. When a member flies United, JetBlue still earns the points and co-brand economics. The switch is not a full revenue loss.
- **Status inertia anecdote (AJOT, above):** a passenger whose United Newark flight was cancelled after an 8-hour delay said she would keep flying United because of her premier status. Loyalty status holds customers even through operational failure, which works for JetBlue's TrueBlue members too.

## 6. Segment mismatch on the 19 routes

- Roughly 12 of the 19 overlap routes are leisure or visiting-friends-and-relatives Florida/Caribbean/Buffalo markets: SJU, BQN, MCO, FLL, PBI, TPA, RSW, JAX, SAV, CHS, BUF, MSY.
- Per Ishii, leisure flyers weight fare and access, not product (Wi-Fi, on-time), and showed a negative United preference.
- BQN and SJU are JetBlue strongholds with a Puerto Rican diaspora concentrated in NYC's outer boroughs, the far side of the city from EWR.
- Only the transcon and business routes (LAX, SFO, BOS, DEN, PHX, LAS, RDU) look like plausible switching markets. Business flyers there are the most access-time sensitive (Hess & Polak).

## Best evidence for the pitch (1-3 bullets)
- After the 2025 outages, Kayak and Data Appeal show NYC flyers did move between airports quickly (EWR departures -15%, LGA arrivals +5.7%). The three airports are partly substitutes, and DOT's Table 1 itself treats NYC as one city market.
- United's airline-level on-time rate beat JetBlue's in 2025: 78.77% vs 74.66% (Cirium: https://www.cirium.com/thoughtcloud/most-on-time-airlines-airports-2025-revealed-cirium/). The product gap is real at the airline level.
- Brueckner et al. (2014, Review of Industrial Organization; https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/revind/v44y2014i1p1-25.html) give "strong evidence" that city-pairs, not airport-pairs, are the right market definition for many large metros. EWR and JFK are treated as adjacent airports to LGA in follow-on work.

## Evidence against (the devil's case, ranked)
1. Access time dominates airport choice. A 5-minute change moves share about 20% (Ishii). JFK to EWR is a 45-90 minute and $15-17+ penalty for the JFK catchment.
2. Consumers rank EWR last (Kayak). EWR is less reliable than JFK (cancellations 2.7% vs 1.6%), and it is FAA-capped at 72 operations per hour through Oct 2027, so United cannot add seats there.
3. JFK and EWR fares and competition differ (DOT Table 7: $294 vs $269; 88% vs 66% of passengers in low-fare markets). Fare parity has not been shown at the airport-pair level.
4. JetBlue loyalty revenue rose 20% in Q3 2026. Blue Sky switching keeps JetBlue's points economics, so a "switcher" is not a lost customer.
5. Most overlap routes are leisure or visiting-friends-and-relatives routes, where Wi-Fi and on-time matter less than fare and proximity.

## Suggested haircut and why
- **Count 30% of the 6.48M JFK overlap passengers, about 1.9M.** That puts the base at about **4.3M**, not 8.8M.
- Build-up (illustrative, with the assumptions flagged):
  - About 50% of JFK overlap passengers have a realistic EWR alternative: Manhattan, NJ and Staten Island origins, and transcon business flyers. This is an assumption; origin data was not retrievable.
  - Multiply by about 0.6 for Newark's last-place preference, worse cancellations and the FAA cap through Oct 2027.
  - Result: 0.5 x 0.6 = **0.30**.
- Sensitivity range to show judges: 15% (skeptic, about 1.0M) / 30% (base, about 1.9M) / 50% (generous, about 3.2M).
- **Warning for the pitch.** Linear interpolation from the brief: adding 6.48M moves the return from +8% to -16%, about -3.7 points per 1M passengers. Under that approximation:
  - A 30% haircut gives about **+0.8%**, so the short roughly breaks even on this driver alone.
  - 15% gives about +4.4%.
  - 50% gives about -4%.
  - The short case should therefore not rest on the JFK overlap. Present it as upside and rely on other drivers.
- Better framing a judge would accept: count JFK overlap only where United actually serves JFK under Blue Sky from 2027. That is a true same-airport overlap and avoids the airport-substitution argument entirely.
