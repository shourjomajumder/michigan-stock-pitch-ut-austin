# 1. How antitrust authorities and courts define the New York airline market

Question: do regulators and courts treat JFK, LGA and EWR as one market (a "New York" city pair) or as separate airport pairs? Is there data on substitution between them?

Short answer: there is no single answer. For DOMESTIC routes, the DOJ (2021) and the DOT (2010) both said Newark is NOT a close substitute for JFK/LGA. JetBlue and American argued in court (2023) that "New York" must include Newark. The NEA judge declined to decide. The JetBlue/Spirit judge (2024) counted routes metro-to-metro and noted that airlines plan New York as one city. No court or agency published a diversion ratio between the NYC airports.

Sources were read on 2026-11-11 (WebFetch of public PDFs). Page numbers are the documents' internal pagination unless noted.

---

## 1. DOJ complaint, US v. American Airlines & JetBlue (Northeast Alliance), D. Mass. No. 1:21-cv-11558, filed 2021-09-21
URL: https://www.justice.gov/atr/case-document/file/1434836/dl

- ¶41: geographic markets are "each origin and destination pair where American and JetBlue compete."
- ¶42: an endpoint is "no broader than all the airports in a metropolitan area." Airports inside one metro area can still be separate markets because ground access differs and airlines price them differently.
- ¶45 (DOMESTIC): JFK/LGA is treated as separate from Newark. DOJ says "many passengers traveling to and from JFK or LaGuardia" do not see Newark as a reasonable substitute. It quotes JetBlue's own description of Newark's catchment area as **"distinct."**
- ¶46 (TRANSATLANTIC): JFK, LGA and EWR together "may" be reasonable substitutes. Longer trips that involve customs make passengers more willing to switch airports.
- Appendix B: the share tables use the grouping "NYC (JFK/LGA)" with Newark left out. Example from 2019 revenue: NYC-Boston, American 29% + JetBlue 21% = 50%, with an HHI increase of 1,207.

So what: this is the strongest evidence AGAINST the thesis. All 19 of the pitch's JFK routes are domestic (SJU and BQN are Puerto Rico, which counts as domestic). That puts them under the ¶45 treatment (not substitutes), not ¶46.

## 2. Judge Sorokin, Findings of Fact & Conclusions of Law, US v. American Airlines Group & JetBlue, 2023-05-19
URL (public copy): https://uschamber.com/assets/documents/District-Court-Decision.pdf

- p. 75: the parties agreed on the product market, "scheduled air passenger service." Geographic markets are origin-and-destination pairs (O&Ds).
- p. 76: the government's expert, Dr. Miller, limited the New York endpoint to routes "beginning or ending at JFK or LaGuardia only." **JetBlue and American argued that "New York" must include Newark.** Their expert, Dr. Brueckner, testified only on that point (cited in n.64).
- p. 76: the court called it "much ado about nothing." It said "both sides might be right" and that it "need not choose between the two."
- p. 77 (citing PX 0885): the defendants' combined share was "exceeding thirty percent at JFK and LaGuardia" and "twenty-five percent in the region with Newark included."
- p. 10: "Newark is a major hub for United's domestic and international service."
- pp. 39-40 n.52: when awarding Newark slots, DOT said the NEA put JetBlue in a position to keep or grow its "access in NYC." This treats Newark as part of JetBlue's NYC access.

So what: JetBlue itself, through its counsel and an expert, has argued on the court record that New York includes Newark. That makes a useful point against management. The court did not adopt the argument, though.

## 3. Judge Young, Findings of Fact & Conclusions of Law, US v. JetBlue & Spirit, No. 23-10511-WGY, 2024-01-16
URL: https://www.justice.gov/atr/media/1380311/dl

- p. 16 n.19: "Airlines often refer to a metropolitan area as one city" because customers "have the choice to fly out of more than one airport." Its examples are New York (LGA, EWR, JFK), Miami/Fort Lauderdale, and Orlando (MCO/SFB).
- p. 43: "As measured by metropolitan area, JetBlue and Spirit have 99 nonstop overlap routes." The court counted overlaps metro to metro, so a JetBlue JFK flight overlapping a Spirit EWR or LGA flight counted.
- p. 72: the market is "each individual O&D pair, otherwise known as a route." On p. 70 the court reasons from consumers going from one "metropolitan area" to another.
- p. 55: the defense criticised the government expert for failing to "group airports from the same metropolitan area." The court said some of the defense objections were correct but still gave his work "significant weight."
- p. 30: "Both LaGuardia and Newark are slot- and gate-constrained airports."

So what: this is the best precedent FOR the thesis. A federal court counted JetBlue's NYC competition at the metro level and described the New York airports as one city for airline planning. However, the court did not make a specific finding on how much JFK and EWR substitute for each other.

## 4. DOT notice on the Delta/US Airways LGA-DCA slot swap, 75 Fed. Reg. (2010-02-18), FR Doc. 2010-3109
URL: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2010-02-18/pdf/2010-3109.pdf

- DOT used ticket data to conclude that the NYC airports do not "effectively constitute the same market for all passengers."
- Quantitative evidence of imperfect substitution (yield, i.e. revenue per passenger mile):
  - LGA 20.5 cents
  - EWR 18.7 cents (about 9% below LGA)
  - JFK 14.7 cents (about 28% below LGA)
  - Trips under 1,000 miles: LGA passengers pay "23% more" than JFK passengers ($147 vs $120 one way).
- DOT's reasoning: if the airports were full substitutes, yields would converge much more than they do.
- DOJ comments on the same deal (https://www.justice.gov/atr/public/comments/257463.pdf, p. 16): other airports "may be acceptable substitutes for some passengers (particularly price-sensitive passengers)." For others they "clearly are not close substitutes."

So what: this cuts both ways. The fare gaps show the airports are not one market. But DOJ explicitly says price-sensitive passengers do substitute, and those are exactly the base-fare JetBlue customers the thesis targets.

## 5. DOJ, United/Continental merger clearance, 2010-08-27
URL: https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/2010/August/10-at-974.html

- DOJ's concern was overlaps between United hubs and Continental's Newark hub, where Continental had "a high share of service" and slots were limited. The remedy was transferring Newark slots and assets to Southwest.
- JetBlue/Spirit defendants' Proposed Conclusions of Law (ECF 446, p. 11 ¶32; https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.mad.254267/gov.uscourts.mad.254267.446.0.pdf) quote the government's position that service to and from Newark "constitutes a relevant antitrust market."

So what: in that review DOJ treated Newark as its own market, which goes against the thesis.

## Not found / gaps
- I found no published diversion ratio, survey or cross-elasticity between JFK and EWR in any of these cases. Dr. Miller's and Dr. Brueckner's underlying analyses (PX 0885) are not public in full.
- DOT's 2021 NEA review agreement was not checked in detail. Its proxy access to transportation.gov was blocked (403).

## Best evidence for the pitch
- **JetBlue's own litigation position:** in the NEA trial, JetBlue and American argued that "New York" must include Newark (Sorokin op., p. 76). The pitch can say: "JetBlue told a federal court that Newark competes with JFK."
- **JetBlue/Spirit (2024):** the court counted overlap routes "as measured by metropolitan area" (p. 43) and noted that airlines treat NYC's LGA, EWR and JFK as one city (n.19).
- **DOJ 2010:** other NYC airports are acceptable substitutes for price-sensitive passengers (DOJ comments, p. 16). That is the base-fare segment the Blue Sky thesis targets.

## Evidence against
- **DOJ 2021 NEA complaint ¶45:** for domestic travel, JFK/LGA is a separate market from Newark, and JetBlue itself described Newark's catchment as "distinct." All 19 routes in the pitch are domestic.
- **DOT 2010:** NYC airports are not the same market "for all passengers." Yields at JFK, EWR and LGA differ by 9-28%, which would not persist if passengers switched freely.
- **No court adopted NYC = JFK+LGA+EWR.** Sorokin declined to choose. Young's metro framing is about counting routes, not a finding of how much JFK and EWR substitute.

## Suggested haircut and why
- Do not count the 6.48M JFK passengers at 100%. The regulator record says "many" domestic JFK/LGA flyers won't use EWR, while price-sensitive flyers will.
- **Suggested base case: count 50% (about 3.2M), with sensitivity at 25% and 100%.**
- Justification: DOJ's "many passengers" (not "most") together with its concession on price-sensitive flyers supports a partial overlap. Bounding at 50% also keeps the pitch defensible against the ¶45 "distinct catchment" quote.
- If other agents find catchment or diversion data (for example, the share of JFK O&D passengers living in NJ or west/lower Manhattan), use that figure in place of the 50% judgment call.
