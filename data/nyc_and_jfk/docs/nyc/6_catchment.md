# 6. Catchment / passenger origin: JFK vs EWR vs LGA

Question: what share of JetBlue's JFK passengers live or stay somewhere that Newark is a realistic alternative?

Bottom line: there is no recent public JFK origin table. (The Port Authority's Air Passenger Survey microdata and the JFK Redevelopment EA Appendix E are not public in readable form.) But several public sources box the answer in:
- **Manhattan** is about half of the close-in airports' market.
- **New Jersey** is overwhelmingly Newark's market already.
- **Brooklyn, Queens and Long Island**, JFK's natural catchment, are where Newark is not a realistic option.

A defensible haircut is to count **about 40-45% of the 6.48M JFK pax** as switchable. That is roughly 2.6-2.9M, not 6.48M.

## Evidence (with sources)

### 2. Port of NY Authority in-flight survey, Sept 1954 (only public 3-airport origin table found; old but structurally telling)
Source: "New York's Air Travelers" (Eno Foundation, 1956), Tables 7 and 11. Some OCR cells were reconstructed. URL: https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/16280/dot_16280_DS1.pdf

| Origin | LGA | NY Intl (now JFK) | Newark |
|---|---|---|---|
| Manhattan | 58% | **52%** | 36% |
| Brooklyn + Queens | 13% | 17% | 4% |
| Nassau + Suffolk | 9% | 10% | 2% |
| NJ (west of Hudson) | 8% | 8% | **50%** |

Airport choice by residents (Table 11):

| Residents of | LGA | JFK | EWR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Manhattan | 63% | 24% | **13%** |
| Brooklyn | 55% | 35% | 10% |
| Queens | 63% | 31% | 6% |
| Essex NJ | 25% | 13% | 62% |

So cross-Hudson use is real for Manhattan and Bergen, and marginal for Queens and Nassau.

### 3. West-of-Hudson residents already choose EWR over JFK at 83.5%
Source: ITF/OECD Discussion Paper 2013-01 ("Upgrading to World Class", NY airports), p. 16. URL: https://www.itf-oecd.org/sites/default/files/docs/dp201301.pdf
- Among passengers based west of the Hudson, the share "who use EWR over JFK is 83.5 percent".
- So the NJ slice of JFK's traffic is small. Most NJ flyers who would switch to Newark already fly from it.
- Table 1, 2012 domestic passengers by airport: United 82.9% at EWR and 5.0% at JFK; JetBlue 79.4% at JFK and 11.1% at EWR. The carriers have largely segmented the airports. JetBlue does already sell about 11% of its NY domestic seats at EWR.

### 4. NYC-resident skew of JFK + LGA
Source: NYC Public Advocate, "Improving Transit Access to NYC Airports" (Nov 2016). URL: https://a860-gpp.nyc.gov/downloads/5d86p0801?locale=en
- About three quarters of JFK + LGA passengers travel to or from the five boroughs.
- Access mode at JFK: 41% taxi/car service, 27% dropped off, 24% transit, 9% drove themselves.
- Union Square to JFK T5 by transit takes about 1h10.
- Battery Park to JFK by taxi at 4pm takes about 63 min (range 45-82).

### 5. Midtown travel times to each airport

| From Midtown | JFK | EWR | LGA |

| Taxi | 40-60 min; $70 flat fare plus fees | 45-60 min; $60-80 metered plus $20 surcharge and tolls | Times Square to LGA avg 35 min, 95th pct 53 (2017 TLC) |
| Free-flow drive (ACRP Rpt 4, 2008) | "in theory" 25 min, several hours worst case | about 18 miles, about 30 min | about 9 miles, about 17 min |

Sources:
- aerocorner.com/blog/jfk-vs-newark/ (fares listed as of Oct 2026)
- ACRP Report 4 Ch. 4: https://crp.trb.org/acrp0715/wp-content/themes/acrp-child/documents/015/original/ACRP_4_Ground_Access_to_Major_Airports_by_Public_Transportation.pdf
- LGA Access Report section 3.2

What this means for Manhattan:
- From Midtown West or Penn Station, EWR and JFK are about equal, and EWR is a one-seat NJT ride to the airport station.
- From Lower Manhattan, PATH to EWR takes about 45 min.
- From the East Side or Upper Manhattan, JFK/LGA win.
- From Brooklyn, Queens or Long Island, EWR adds roughly 45-90 min and a Hudson crossing. It is not a realistic substitute.

### 6. Natural experiment: Newark outages, spring 2025
Source: AJOT/Reuters, "Demand, traffic falls at Newark airport following outages" (May 2025). URL: https://www.ajot.com/news/demand-traffic-falls-at-newark-airport-following-outages
- Kayak: Newark chosen 19% less for arrivals and about 15% less for departures after Apr 28, 2025.
- Data Appeal: EWR arrivals down about 13% YoY (Apr 28-May 20), while LGA arrivals rose 5.7%.
- So travelers do swap between NY airports quickly. In this case they moved *away* from EWR.
- The Kayak CEO's ranking: LGA first, "then JFK and then Newark".

## Proposed haircut (worked estimate)
Assumed JetBlue-JFK origin mix. This is an assumption, built from LGA 2017, NYPA 1954 JFK data and the 83.5% NJ figure, with JFK skewed toward Queens, Brooklyn and Long Island versus LGA:

| Segment | Assumed share of JFK O&D pax | Realistic EWR substitute? | Switchable weight | Contribution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Manhattan (residents + visitors) | 40% | Yes for West/Midtown/Downtown; partly for East/Upper | 0.75 | 30% |
| NJ + Staten Island + Rockland/Orange | 8% | Yes, EWR is closer | 1.0 | 8% |
| Bronx / Westchester / CT / upstate | 12% | Weak (LGA or JFK closer) | 0.25 | 3% |
| Brooklyn + Queens | 25% | No | 0.05 | 1% |
| Long Island | 15% | No | 0.0 | 0% |
| **Total** | 100% | | | **about 42%** |

Applied to 6.48M, this gives **about 2.7M switchable JFK pax**. Range: 2.3M (35%) to 3.2M (50%). The model base becomes about 2.34 + 2.7 = **about 5.0M**, not 8.8M.

Additional reasons to stay at the low end:
- **SJU/BQN VFR (visiting friends and relatives) traffic** is concentrated in the Bronx, Brooklyn and Queens, so less of it is switchable.
- **JFK connecting passengers** on JetBlue's network are not switchable at all.

## Best evidence for the pitch
- Manhattan is about half of the close-in NY airport market: 48.6% at LGA in 2017, and 52% at JFK in 1954. From Midtown West, EWR (NJT about 30 min from Penn) and JFK (LIRR + AirTrain about 35-45 min) are about equal, so a large group of passengers really is airport-indifferent.
- The 2025 Newark outages show NY flyers move between airports within weeks (EWR down 13-19%, LGA up 5.7%). The three airports behave as one market at the margin.
- Even in 1954, 13% of Manhattan residents and 10% of Brooklyn residents chose Newark. Cross-Hudson choice is long-standing.

## Evidence against
- West-of-Hudson flyers already pick EWR over JFK 83.5% of the time. JetBlue's JFK base is mostly east-of-Hudson people, for whom EWR is the *least* preferred airport (Kayak: LGA, then JFK, then Newark).
- Brooklyn, Queens and Long Island (likely about 40% of JFK) face 45-90 extra minutes and a Hudson crossing to reach EWR, so they are effectively non-switchable.
- No public modern JFK origin survey was found. The JFK mix above is inferred, and judges may press on it.
- The airports are carrier-segmented: United is 83% EWR and JetBlue 79% JFK. Blue Sky lets JetBlue members book United, but those members must change airport, not just airline.

## Suggested haircut and why
Count **about 40-45% of the 6.48M** (about 2.6-2.9M) as switchable, and present 35% / 50% as sensitivity bounds. The reasoning:
- About 40% of JFK O&D traffic is Manhattan, and about 75% of that is airport-indifferent between EWR and JFK.
- About 8% is NJ / Staten Island, all of it switchable.
- About 3% comes from the northern suburbs, and essentially none from Brooklyn, Queens or Long Island.

If the team wants one cleaner citation-backed number, use **Manhattan share ≈ 50%** (LGA 2017: 48.6%; JFK 1954: 52%) as the ceiling. Haircut at least 50% for that reason alone.
