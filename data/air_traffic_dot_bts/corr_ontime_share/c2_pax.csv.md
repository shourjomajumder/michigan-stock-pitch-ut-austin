c2_pax: 276 rows, 5 metric series (keep them separate, as they use different definitions).
Coverage: dom_pax_millions (BTS T-100 domestic scheduled enplanements, top-10 only) 2012-2015, 2017-2018 for AA/DL/UA/WN/B6/AS, plus NK 2014-15 and 2017-18 and F9 2017-18. sys_pax_millions_bts (top-10 system) 2012-13 and 2015-18. sys_enpl_millions_mit_form41 2005-09 for 10 carriers (no SY/VX). sys_pax_millions_wiki (Wikipedia key-trends tables, company-reported) roughly 2010/2015-2025 for all except DL.
Gaps: BTS stopped publishing carrier rankings after 2018 (2019+ releases have only totals), and 2016 domestic is missing. HA/G4/SY/VX are never in the BTS top 10. DL has no data for 2010-11 or 2019-25 (Wikipedia has no passenger row; the 10-K fetch was truncated, and it says only "over 190 million customers" for 2023). SY/G4/HA have no data before 2014 except the MIT 2005-09 rows. VX covers only 2012-15.
Caveats: Pre-2015 BTS AA excludes US Airways, and WN 2012-14 excludes AirTran. Wiki AA/UA/AS include regional partners (AA 215M vs BTS ~150M), and WN wiki is revenue passengers rather than enplanements. AS wiki jumps 2015-16 (Virgin America) and in 2025 (Hawaiian). The MIT 2010+ values look ~3x inflated, so they were excluded. The 2011 T-100 market table disagrees with the 2012 segment values, so it is kept under a separate metric.
For a share regression, use within-source series: the BTS dom/sys series for the big 6, and the wiki series for ULCCs, with year fixed effects. Don't mix the wiki AA/UA (regional-inclusive) with the BTS figures.
