a1_delta_alaska: Delta entry at Seattle vs Alaska, 2010-2019. Data: DOT Table 6 (yj5y-b2ir), 15 SEA city pairs pulled 2010Q1-2019Q4; Delta launch dates from press releases (URLs in CSV). k0 = quarter of the first Delta nonstop (next quarter if fewer than 15 days remained). Loss = Alaska share vs the same quarter of the pre-event year. Eventual = mean of k9-12 (k8-10 for 2017 launches and SFO).
Big entry (3-6 daily at launch; 10 routes: SFO SAN PDX GEG PHX BOI SMF DEN PSC EUG). 6 routes show a real Alaska loss (SAN 14, GEG 14, BOI 26, SMF 8.5, PSC 17, EUG 31 pp; mean 17). First effect is in k0 on 5 of 6 (SMF k1). 50% of the 12-quarter loss: median k1 (range 0-4). 90%: median k6.5 (range 1-11). Group-mean path as % of eventual: 46% k0, 73% k1, 89% k2, about 90% through k5, peak 113% at k8, 91% at k12.
Big entry with no measurable loss: SFO, PHX, DEN (all multi-carrier markets, Alaska share flat). PDX was flat for 8 quarters, then lost about 4 pp from k9 onward (more by 2019).
Slow ramp (LAX 3 to 7 daily; LAS 1 to 5 daily; AUS 1 daily; ORD 3 daily; n=4). No measurable loss on LAX, AUS, ORD (within about ±3 pp of noise). Group mean 12-quarter loss is 2.2 pp. LAS lost about 5 pp, with 50% at k1 and 90% at k10, but it is noisy. Quarters to 50% and 90% are not defined for this group.
Shape: on the routes that lost, the loss is a step at launch plus a slow drift. GEG kept falling past k12: Alaska went from 99% to 75% by 2019Q2. BOI partly reversed from 2018Q1. Within 12 quarters, no route shows a clean decay back to the pre-entry share.
Loyalty-driven part (T-100 excess passenger share beyond seat share, DL vs AS minus a UA control; k0 = 2013Q2 hub-build start; pre-event mean -0.41 pp, SD about 0.4):
  onset: one-off +0.93 at k0, sustained from k4 (2014Q2, +1.5 vs pre). Peak: quarterly +1.94 vs pre at k6 (2014Q4); 4-quarter average peak +1.25 at k9 (2015Q3). Decay: 4-quarter average halves by k12 (2016Q2) and reaches about 0 by k15-16 (2017Q1-Q2). That is before the May 2017 partnership end. After the break it runs below the 2013 level (-0.8 in 2018) but only about -0.4 below the 2012 baseline.
Timing summary: capacity-driven loss is front-loaded (50% about k1, 90% about k6). The loyalty or product excess takes about 4 quarters to appear, peaks at k6-9, and fades to zero by about k15. It is small: 1-1.5 pp of passenger share relative to seat share.
Caveats: Table 6 reports only the largest and the lowest-fare carrier. Alaska's share is missing in quarters when it was neither (DEN, ORD, and SLC before 2014Q4; SLC was excluded because Delta was the incumbent). Shares are city-market and include connections. From 2017 on, Alaska's share on SFO, LAX, SAN and LAS may include Virgin America. "Big vs slow" is based on announced frequencies, not T-100 seats. LAS had about 1 daily Delta flight before 2014 (start date not seen). The ORD start year comes from the source URL only. There are 4-6 effective routes per group, so the timing medians are rough.
