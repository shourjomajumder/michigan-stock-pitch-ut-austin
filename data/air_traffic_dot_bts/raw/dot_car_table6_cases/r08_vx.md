- Found: DOT Consumer Airfare Report (SODA 4f3n-jbg2, Q2 snapshots 2007-2019) gives Virgin America share/fare whenever VX was the low-fare carrier, plus the largest incumbent; 39 rows across 11 city pairs in two cases (VX product-only entry, depth 0; Alaska absorbing VX 2017->2018/19, coded depth 3).
- Parity routes (gap <=5% at start): 5 - SFO-SEA 2010 (3.1%), SFO-LAS 2012 (3.2%), SFO-WAS 2013 (3.1%), BOS-SFO 2017 (3.0%), AUS-SFO 2017 (1.0%). Everywhere else VX was 13-30% cheaper.
- Pattern: VX's better product moved share only 1-4 pts over 2-4 years, and mostly while discounting; at parity it stalled or lost (SFO-LAS -3, SFO-WAS -2 while hub carrier UA +9). After Alaska took over, share rose where AS cut fares/added network (NYC-SF 16.1->20.5, AUS-SF 16.1->24.3, DFW-SF 11.5->14.0) but fell on BOS-SF and LA-NYC; SFO-SEA combined AS+VX held 58.5->58.4 then slid to 53.3.
- Caveats: city-pair (metro) not airport-pair; only two carriers visible per route-quarter, so VX is missing in quarters it was neither largest nor cheapest; shares are O&D passengers incl. connections; fares are average itinerary fares with cabin mix (UA transcon premium-heavy); pre-2015 shares rounded to whole pts; values transcribed via a fetch tool, spot-check before publishing.
- DOJ CIS (FR 2016-29883) has no route-level shares (only: VX overlaps AA on 20 nonstop routes, ~$8B commerce; remedy cuts AS-AA codeshare); UBC paper (Lambelet 2025) reports fares +$40.96 after the VX brand was eliminated in 2018, no share numbers.
