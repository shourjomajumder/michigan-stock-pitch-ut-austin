# a3: American-Alaska West Coast International Alliance (WCIA), Seattle overlap routes, Table 6 quarterly 2017Q1-2026Q1
- Dates: announced Feb 2020. Full reciprocal earning (domestic and international) from 1 Apr 2020 (samchui.com, 2 Apr 2020), so k=0 is 2020Q2. Alaska joined oneworld 31 Mar 2021 (news.alaskaair.com), which is k=4 (2021Q2). Alaska elite perks on AA (preferred seats, upgrades) arrived spring to late 2021.
- Overlap routes, AS share where Table 6 shows it: SEA-PHL, DFW, MIA, CHI, NYC, BOS. Measure: AS share minus same quarter 2019, net of the same change on control routes (AS share on SEA-DEN, ATL, SLC, LAS, LAX). The partner (AA) is the "loser"; rivals DL and UA are tracked on SEA-ATL, MSP, SLC and IAH.
- Pooled DiD by year, in pp: 2017 -5.1, 2018 -5.3, 2019 0, 2020 +9.7, 2021 +1.3, 2022 +9.4, 2023 +5.5, 2024 +4.2, 2025 +2.2, 2026Q1 -0.6. Excluding ATL from the controls: 2022 +12.3, 2023 +8.7, 2024 +6.6, 2025 +4.0, 2026Q1 +1.2.
- COVID: the k=0-1 spike (+19 pp, SEA-PHL +34) is AA cutting capacity while AS kept flying. Controls also spiked (+10 pp in 2021Q1). k=0-6 cannot be read as switching.
- Timing, using the 4-quarter rolling DiD after COVID. First clean effect: k=7 (2022Q1, +3 to +5 pp), the first quarter after COVID that can be read, and 3 quarters after oneworld and elite perks. 50%: k=8 (2022Q2). 90%: k=9-10. Peak: k=10 (2022Q4), +9.3 pp (+12.1 ex-ATL), 6 quarters after oneworld.
- Decay: the effect halved about 6 quarters after the peak (k=16, +4.6). It was +1 to +3 pp by k=23 (2026Q1). On SEA-DFW it went negative from 2024Q3 as AA retook largest-carrier status in 2025Q2. On SEA-MIA and SEA-BOS, Delta became the largest carrier in some quarters of 2024-26.
- Partner (AA) share on SEA-PHL vs the same quarter of 2019: Q2 change -16.0 (2021), -18.7 (2024), -16.7 (2025); Q3 change -1 to -4 and +1.6 (2025Q3). The partner-to-partner shift is persistent but seasonal.
- Rivals vs the same quarter of 2019. DL on SEA-ATL: worst -14.3 (2023Q2), recovering to -0.1 to -6.3 in 2025. DL on SEA-SLC: about -5 to -10 in 2023-25. DL on SEA-MSP: -5 to -9 in 2023-24. UA on SEA-IAH: -1.4 to -5.7 in 2022-25. These routes have no AA, so the declines reflect Alaska and Delta hub competition, not the alliance.
- Caveats. Table 6 shows only the largest carrier and the lowest-fare carrier, so AA is mostly unobserved after 2020 and the panel is unbalanced (n = 4-6 routes per quarter). There is no seat data, so capacity cannot be separated from loyalty (AA trimmed SEA flying, and Delta and AA later added back). There is a pre-2019 step: AS was already 5 pp lower in 2017-18, and the gain came in 2019, before the announcement. LA-area routes are not usable: AS is unobserved on LA-BOS and drops out of view on LA-NYC after 2020Q1 (capacity exit).
- Single best-supported number: after COVID, the alliance partner with the local network gained about +9 pp of share over the partner, peaking about 6 quarters after the full benefits were in place and roughly halving within the next 1.5 years.
