Constrained-airspace exposure (for item 3):
- JBLU: ~951k June-2026 New York departing seats, 83% of them at JFK. FLL is 432k seats (33.9% airport share) (Aviation Week / OAG).
- Against an ESTIMATED ~3.4m monthly system seats, that puts JBLU at roughly 28% NYC + 13% FLL. Adding BOS (not found, est. ~15–20%) gives roughly 55–60% of JBLU capacity in NYC/BOS/FLL airspace.
- ULCC: DEN is its HQ and largest base, but its DEN share was not found (est. ~15–20%). ULCC is spread across MCO, LAS and other markets, so I judge its congestion exposure to be well under JBLU's.

Takeaways
- Every one of these shocks hits JBLU harder per dollar of equity. The short leg is 1.163× the long and JBLU has a smaller cap and higher costs, so on every adverse shock the pair is net LONG protection: the pair gains.
- The flip side: wage disinflation, a weaker USD, smooth ATC, or tariff relief each hurt the pair by the same amounts.
- Labor is by far the biggest exposure, about 14.5% per $1 for a 5% wage move. JBLU's SWB alone is about 3× its market cap. It is also the most live risk: JBLU's SWB rose 5.8% in FY25 and ULCC's rose 6%.
- FX is small and mostly a JBLU-only exposure (Europe plus some Latin America point-of-sale). Neither company reports material foreign-currency costs; fuel and aircraft are priced in USD.
- The ~+24% sum assumes the shocks happen together, which is unrealistic. Treat each row as its own scenario.

Caveats
- Hits are one-year operating income ÷ cap with no multiple, tax or pass-through. In practice airlines pass part of wage and tariff cost on to fares, and ULCC's fare elasticity is higher.
- ULCC geographic split, both DEN/BOS shares, JBLU shutdown $, share counts and the tariff base are ASSUMED or ESTIMATED. Check them against the FY25 10-Ks (ULCC accession 0001670076-26-000020).
- Tariffs: under the 2025 US-EU framework (as I recall it; not re-checked here) aircraft and parts were largely exempt, so the tariff row is a tail risk, not a base case. GTF engines (Pratt, US) are a supply risk for both: JBLU's 2026 plan assumes mid-single-digit aircraft on the ground (AOG).
- Labor uses FY25 SWB; 2026 run-rates are higher. JBLU's cost base is also more unionised (pilots and flight attendants), which adds contract-reset risk.
