## Read
- The leverage gap leaves the pair **short credit/rate risk**. JBLU carries 17x ULCC's debt on a smaller market cap, and ULCC is net cash. Each +100bp takes roughly 2.5-3.5% of market cap a year from JBLU's pre-tax earnings, against about 0.3% for ULCC. After the 1.163 ratio, the pair gains about +2.7% to +3.7% of notional in earnings terms.
- This agrees in sign with the HYG beta (-1.06 per $1 of pair; +5.3% in the HYG -5% scenario). It conflicts with the near-zero 10y beta, which suggests the equity market prices JBLU's risk through spreads, not Treasury yields.
- The real tail is spread, not base rate. JBLU's TrueBlue bonds trade at ~47 (yield >30%, AJOT, 10/26). Refinancing ~$1.06bn of 2027-28 maturities near ~13% instead of ~6-7% would cost ~$65-75m/yr (illustrative), about 6% of JBLU's market cap. That is ~7% for the pair.

## Caveats

- ULCC's balance-sheet debt understates its leverage. Aircraft operating leases (rent ~$1.1bn/yr) reprice at lease renewal through lessor rates, and that effect is not modeled. On a 7x-rent lease-adjusted basis, ULCC leverage is ~10x+ (rough), and EBITDAR is depressed by restructuring charges.
- JBLU's floating share ranges from $2.0bn (company figure) to $3.5bn (instrument labels). The JBLU maturities are from press reporting of the 10-Q, not the filing itself. The JBLU 3Q 10-Q was not fetched directly.
- EBITDAR is 9M x 4/3, includes special items, and is not company-reported. Market caps use the 11/2 close.
